# Plan: order ahead for a pickup time

## Why

Customers ask to order on Friday for a Saturday cake, and today they cannot. Every online order is for now: the page says the order is ready as soon as it is baked, and the counter's board lists orders in the order they arrived. The counter staff took 41 order-ahead requests by phone last month and wrote them on paper.

## What changes

1. The order page gets a pickup time choice: as soon as possible, which is today's behaviour and stays the default, or a day and an hour.
2. The counter's board lists orders by pickup time, not by when they arrived. Orders for later days sit under a "Later" heading.
3. The baker's morning list is the board for that day, grouped by hour.
4. The customer gets one text message on the morning of pickup. The text we send today when an order is on the counter stays.

## What stays

Paying at pickup stays. Orders for now work exactly as today.

## Decisions for the owners

- How far ahead may a customer order? We recommend up to two days. A week would let customers plan parties, but the baker cannot plan flour that far.
- Should a customer pay when they order ahead, or at pickup? We recommend at pickup, as today. Paying online needs a card service, which is a second project.

## The work, in order

(1) the pickup time on the order page; (2) the board by pickup time; (3) the baker's morning list; (4) the morning text.

## Costs

The morning text uses the text service we already pay for, one more message per order ahead.

## Risks

- Too many orders for one hour on a Saturday. We would cap the orders per hour, and say so on the page when an hour is full.
- Customers who order ahead and do not come. If more than one in ten orders ahead are not picked up in the first month, we stop order ahead and talk to the owners.
- The board's new order confuses the counter for a week. The counter staff see it before it goes live.
